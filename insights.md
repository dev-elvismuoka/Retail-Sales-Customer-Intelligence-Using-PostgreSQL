# Business Insights & Recommendations

## 1. High Revenue vs. High Margin Categories (Q33)
* **The finding (Q33):** The `Electronics` category (e.g., Smart TVs, Soundbars) generates the highest total revenue, but `Accessories` (e.g., Earbuds, Chargers, Mice) yield a much higher profit margin percentage.
* **Why it matters:** Depending solely on low-margin, high-revenue electronics ties up working capital and leaves the business vulnerable to supplier price increases. High-margin accessories are the true drivers of baseline profitability.
* **Recommendation:** Implement a cross-selling strategy at checkout. For every laptop or mobile phone purchased, automatically offer a 10% discount on a related accessory (like a mouse or a 20W charger) to boost the overall profit margin of the order.

## 2. Customer Churn Risk (Q34)
* **The finding (Q34):** A significant number of registered customers have not completed an order in over 90 days compared to the latest order date in the database.
* **Why it matters:** In retail, acquiring a new customer is significantly more expensive than retaining an existing one. A 90-day lapse in electronics and home appliances indicates these customers might be shifting to competitors for their everyday tech needs.
* **Recommendation:** Launch an automated "Win-Back" email and SMS campaign targeted at this specific list of customers, offering them a limited-time free delivery voucher or a 15% discount on their next purchase to re-engage them.

## 3. Geographic Sales Concentration (Q25)
* **The finding (Q25):** Revenue is heavily skewed towards major urban centers like Nairobi and Mombasa, with towns like Machakos, Nyeri, and Meru contributing a much smaller fraction of total completed sales.
* **Why it matters:** Duka Smart Electronics is paying overhead to operate and market in ten towns, but failing to capture adequate market share in the secondary towns, leading to an unbalanced growth pipeline.
* **Recommendation:** Run targeted, localized social media promotions and subsidized delivery days specifically in the underperforming secondary towns to build brand awareness and capture market share outside of the capital.

## 4. The Cost of Cancelled Orders (Q20)
* **The finding (Q20):** A considerable percentage of total orders end up with a 'Cancelled' status rather than 'Completed'.
* **Why it matters:** Cancelled orders represent lost revenue, wasted administrative effort, and tied-up inventory that could have been sold to other ready buyers. 
* **Recommendation:** Analyze the cancelled orders further to see if they are linked to a specific payment method (like Cash on Delivery) or high-ticket items. Introduce a mandatory 10% upfront deposit for high-value items (like TVs and Laptops) to ensure buyer intent before dispatching inventory.

## 5. Segment Spending Disparities (Q27)
* **The finding (Q27):** The average spending of `VIP` and `Corporate` customer segments vastly outpaces the average spending of standard `Retail` customers.
* **Why it matters:** While VIPs are highly lucrative, relying on a small pool of high-spenders introduces systemic risk if a few of them stop buying. The standard Retail segment has the highest volume of people but the lowest basket size.
* **Recommendation:** Create a tier-based loyalty program for standard Retail buyers. Gamify their experience by offering points for every purchase, incentivizing them to increase their average order value and eventually "graduate" into higher spending brackets.
